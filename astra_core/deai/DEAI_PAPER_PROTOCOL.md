# DEAI Paper Protocol — v3.0 (2026-10-17)

**v3.0 changes (post external review by ChatGPT and Claude):** the v3.0
addendum in `DEAI_v3_ADDENDUM.md` is part of this protocol and takes
precedence over any conflicting v2.0 clause. Its central corrections:
Class A (correctness, mandatory) vs Class B (diagnostic, flag-only) test
separation; default-to-preserve with minimal-edit preference; audit of the
reviser's own new text (B-29) and edit budget (B-33); coined-terminology
register; segmentation validation before sentence metrics; LaTeX/PDF/file
hygiene, figure/table/equation/acronym, house-style, statement, literature,
overlap and reader checks; editing strictly separated from refereeing
(statistical, provenance and reproducibility findings go to the referee
report and author decisions, never into silent edits); machine-verifiable
execution manifest with PASS/REVIEW/NOT-APPLICABLE/NOT-RUN/FAIL statuses.

Operational instructions for removing machine-generated prose patterns from a
finished scientific manuscript (MNRAS-style astronomy papers), while strictly
preserving the science. Merges the CLAUDE "Manuscript Style and Reasoning
Audit" v1.0 and the ChatGPT "Scientific Manuscript Audit and
Evidence-Preserving Revision" v1.0.

**Trigger:** run ONLY when the author explicitly asks, typically near the end
of the paper-writing process. Never run it as part of routine iterations.

**Inputs the author provides:** the rendered `.pdf` of the paper (read this to
see the paper as a reader/referee sees it) and the source tree: main `.tex`,
any included/subsidiary `.tex` files, `.bib`, table files, figure files, and
class/style files.

**Standing project rules that override everything below:**
- Version stamp is bumped FIRST (new version directory/file), and every prior
  version of tex/pdf/scripts is kept. Never rename or delete earlier versions.
- No referee response letters are ever created (standing directive). The
  referee-letter guidance at the end is retained for reference only and is
  used solely if the author explicitly asks for letter text.
- Always recompile (pdflatex + bibtex) and leave a readable `.pdf`.

## 0. Purpose and philosophy

**EXECUTION CONTRACT (v2.0 — binding; learned from the 2026-10-17 first live run,
where surface fixes were applied while construction-level tells went unmeasured
until the author challenged twice):**

1. **Diagnostics before edits.** The full Section 5.13 measured battery is
   computed and written into `deai_audit_report.md` BEFORE any revision. A run
   that edits first and measures later is invalid.
2. **No silent scope reduction.** Every battery row gets a before-value, an
   after-value, and a verdict (fixed / in-range / deliberately kept with
   reason). A row may be waived only with a written justification the author
   could overrule. "Ran out of attention" is not a justification.
3. **Convergence loop.** After the revision pass, the battery is re-run. A
   row outside its threshold is resolved either by a fix or by a waiver that
   quotes the passages concerned and states why they are correct as written;
   forcing a metric into range by edits that make the text worse is itself a
   failure (I-7). The run ends when every row is fixed, in range, or waived
   with a quoted reason (max three cycles).
4. **Relocation guard.** After removing any dominant feature (e.g. em-dashes),
   re-measure the replacement constructions (colons, semicolons, parentheses,
   "rather than", sentence inversions). A fix that drives a replacement above
   its own threshold has not fixed anything.
5. **Depth ordering.** Construction-level and redundancy metrics (sentence
   openings, duplicates, hedge census, statistical restatement) are measured
   and fixed BEFORE word-level polish, so that word edits are not made to
   sentences that will be restructured or deleted.

The goal is a paper that reads as the work of an experienced research
scientist: precise, specific, argued, in the author's voice, and free of the
recognisable habits of machine-generated prose.

The goal is **not** to make the paper look artificially human. Both source
analyses agree, and this protocol adopts the position: a paper reads as
human-written when it does what good scientists do — state measurements
instead of evaluations, take a position, argue with the literature, spend
words on what matters, and keep every number consistent. Most linguistic tells
disappear when the underlying reasoning and emphasis are fixed. **Fix the
reasoning first, the prose second.**

Two questions are worked; a third is never attempted:
1. Is a passage linguistically unusual for professional writing in this field?
2. Does it contain a genuine writing or reasoning defect?
3. *(Was it generated by AI? — never answer this, never report an "AI
   probability". Detectors cannot answer it reliably and flag human writers,
   especially second-language authors.)*

Every flag must name a concrete defect or a measured deviation, never a vibe.

## 1. Invariants (a revision breaking any of these is rejected)

- **I-1 Numbers.** Every number, uncertainty, unit, symbol, object name,
  catalogue ID, equation and table entry survives unchanged unless the change
  is a recorded correction approved by the author.
- **I-2 Citations.** None added, removed or re-attached to a different claim
  without a change-log entry. Never invent a reference.
- **I-3 Evidential strength.** Never strengthen a claim; weakening is allowed
  only to match evidence, and must be flagged for approval.
- **I-4 Meaning.** Every scientific proposition maps to an equivalent
  proposition, except redundancies removed via the change log.
- **I-5 No fabrication.** Never add results, history, motivation, failures or
  anecdotes not in the research record. Missing content is flagged, not
  invented. (This is the single most important rule: no fake humanity.)
- **I-6 Science defects are reported, not papered over** with better wording.
- **I-7 Over-correction ban.** No synonym swaps that break jargon, no slang,
  no deliberate typos or awkwardness, no random fragments, no mechanical
  short/long alternation, no external "humaniser" tools, no removing the
  AI-assistance disclosure required by the journal.

A number string surviving unchanged is not proof of unchanged meaning:
numbers can be silently re-attached to different referents. The token
register diff (Section 3) catches this.

## 2. Stage 0 — preparation

1. Copy the source tree to a fresh version directory; bump the version stamp
   first. Work only on the copy. Originals stay read-only.
2. Read the supplied `.pdf` end-to-end first, as a reader. Note where you
   stumble, where your attention drops, and what the paper's one memorable
   result is (or fails to be).
3. Parse the `.tex` tree (pylatexenc/TexSoup). Segment by section,
   paragraph, sentence (spaCy `en_core_web_trf` where available). Mask math,
   citations, labels, figure/table environments with placeholders; restore
   after revision. Classify each section by function (Abstract, Intro, Data,
   Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions, Appendix, Captions, Ack.).
   Metrics are only ever compared within the same section type.
4. **Protected-token register** (drives I-1/I-2 and the final diff): extract
   every number+unit+uncertainty, symbol, equation, object/catalogue ID,
   citation key, fig/table ref, defined acronym — each with location and
   sentence.
5. **Protected-terms list** (never flag/replace these): radio-quiet,
   radio-loud, quiet Sun, quiescent/quiescence, robust
   estimator/regression (statistical), significant/significance (attached to
   a test, p-value or sigma), novel ("novel method" when demonstrated),
   framework (named software/statistical), landscape (likelihood/parameter),
   demonstrate/reveal (actual demonstration / instrumental "the image
   reveals a shell"), dynamics (physics of gas), silent (instrumental).
   Extend from the manuscript and author's prior usage.
6. **Baselines.** Ideal: the author's pre-2022 papers (voice target) + 50–200
   refereed arXiv astro-ph LaTeX sources 2012–2020 in the same subfield
   (discipline baseline). Report each metric as a percentile and robust
   z = (x − median)/(1.4826·MAD). Corpus A wins on disagreement. If no corpus
   is built (the normal case for a quick pass), use the provisional
   thresholds in Section 5 and mark every flag "uncalibrated".

**Calibration philosophy (from the ChatGPT analysis, adopted):** a feature
outside the reference range is a *review flag*, not a target to be forced
into range. Do not impose mechanical quotas ("one contrast per paper",
"every significance claim needs a number"). Valid technical distinctions,
conceptual significance claims with stated consequences, and short factual
sentences are legitimate and are preserved. The acceptance test is "clearer,
more precise, reads like the author" — not "every metric inside P10–P90".

## 3. Stage 1 — scientific integrity audit (always first, highest priority)

### 3.1 Main-finding test
Write the paper's main finding in ONE sentence containing a number with
uncertainty or a limit, and name the single decisive observation. If this is
impossible ("50+50 equalled 37" — polished prose the reader cannot remember),
that is severity 1: the Abstract, Introduction and Conclusions must be
reorganised around the finding before any prose work. Store the sentence;
it is re-tested at the end.

### 3.2 Argument graph
Nodes: OBSERVATION → MEASUREMENT → UNCERTAINTY → INTERPRETATION →
ALTERNATIVES → MODEL COMPARISON → CONCLUSION. Every paragraph links to at
least one node and performs one of: reports a measurement; describes a
reproducible procedure; defines a quantity/criterion; establishes a
constraint; compares data with prediction; tests an interpretation; rules out
an alternative; identifies a limitation; resolves an inconsistency; motivates
a specific gap; states a quantified consequence. A paragraph doing none of
these is **F-NOFUNC** — candidate for deletion (the commonest case:
restating importance).

### 3.3 Claim–evidence checks
| ID | Defect |
|----|--------|
| E-01 | Claim with no supporting evidence or citation |
| E-02 | Claim inflation across Results → Discussion → Abstract/Conclusions |
| E-03 | Abstract/conclusions stronger than the body |
| E-04 | Circular conclusion restating the selection criterion |
| E-05 | Conclusion extends past the sample ignoring selection effects |
| E-06 | Observation/interpretation/conclusion conflated (O/I/C labels) |
| E-07 | Alternatives listed but not resolved (no "we favour X because…") |
| E-08 | Decisive observation never identified |
| E-09 | Anomalous result reported but not discussed |
| E-10 | Null results / failed tests under-reported vs the research record |
| E-11 | Unverifiable novelty claim ("first", "unprecedented") — check ADS |
| E-12 | "widely accepted" without citation |
| E-13 | Correlation narrated as causation |
| E-14 | Undefined operational terms ("reliable detection", "high-confidence") |
| E-15 | False precision; uncertainties to 1–2 sig figs, value rounded to match |
| E-16 | "uncertainty" not classified statistical/systematic/methodological |
| E-17 | Too-perfect results (χ²_red = 1.0000, identical errors, round numbers) — investigate, do not assume fabrication |
| E-18 | Untraceable observing-log/procedural detail (I-5) |
| E-19 | Artificial optimism: caveats in Results missing from Abstract/Concl. |
| E-20 | Generic future work — must name observation, quantity, hypotheses separated |

### 3.4 Epistemic ladder (wording must match evidence, consistently everywhere)
| Level | Wording | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | we speculate; conceivable | physical argument only |
| 1 | possible; could; may | not excluded by data |
| 2 | consistent with; compatible | data agree, alternatives open |
| 3 | suggests; favours; indicates | data prefer over named alternatives |
| 4 | likely; probably; strong evidence | quantitative preference (σ, likelihood ratio) |
| 5 | demonstrates; establishes; confirms | alternatives excluded quantitatively |

Flag uniform hedging AND uniform confidence, not just mismatches.
"Demonstrate/reveal/show" are not banned; they are checked against the ladder.

### 3.5 Numerical consistency
- N-01 same quantity ⇒ same value/uncertainty/unit everywhere (abstract,
  text, tables, captions, conclusions).
- N-02 counts reconcile (sub-totals, percentages, denominators).
- N-03 no number in two roles; flag exact coincidences for checking.
- N-04 table values physically/logically possible.
- N-05 text matches the research record where one exists (severity 1).
- N-06 definitions stable across sections (sample names, thresholds).
- N-07 fig/table refs point to the right object; panel letters exist.
- The same number in different units is reconciled, not treated as
  contradiction; different denominators are not merged (adversarial cases 5–6).

### 3.6 Astronomy-specific correctness
Flux vs flux density vs intensity vs surface brightness; optically thick/thin;
thermal/non-thermal; spectral-index sign convention stated once and used
consistently; resolution vs astrometric accuracy; sensitivity vs completeness;
primary vs synthesised beam; MNRAS units (km s⁻¹, Jy beam⁻¹, British
spelling); no elementary explanations an expert doesn't need, full
explanation of anything unusual; equations introduced by the quantity they
compute; plausible-sounding-but-wrong mechanisms are the strongest expert
tells — flag for the author when unsure. Methods must be executable
(versions, flags, thresholds, archive IDs) — not "advanced statistical
techniques".

## 4. Stage 2 — citation audit

R-01 Resolve every .bib entry against ADS/Crossref (authors, year, title,
journal, vol, page, DOI/arXiv). Unresolvable ⇒ severity 1. Never guess-repair.
R-02 Claim–source alignment: the cited paper actually supports the specific
sentence (read the source abstract/passage, not just metadata).
R-03 Split 3+-citation blocks so each source supports a specific claim.
R-04 Missing citation on a factual sentence.
R-05 "Numerous studies have…" without naming findings.
R-06 Literature as list, not argument (X found A. Y showed B. — no
disagreement or gap identified).
R-07 Chronology errors; R-08 "independent confirmations" sharing one dataset;
R-09 method credited to an application not the origin; R-10 over-reliance on
a small famous-paper set; R-11 flattery ("seminal", "elegant") — cite plainly.

## 5. Stage 3 — linguistic audit

**Clustering beats single features.** One instance of almost anything is
defensible (and human writers are wrongly accused on single constructions).
What readers notice is several tells together, or one device in every
paragraph. The co-occurrence score (5.10) is the main rewrite trigger.

Provisional thresholds apply when no corpus was built; mark flags
"uncalibrated". "P90" = above the 90th percentile of the discipline corpus.

### 5.1 Lexical (L)
- **L-01** excess-vocabulary lexicon (Kobak et al. 2024 excess words):
  delve, underscore(s), showcase, highlight (evaluative), leverage, harness,
  foster, bolster, elucidate, unravel, unveil, navigate, streamline,
  facilitate, illuminate, unpack, spearhead, garner, pivotal, crucial,
  intricate, comprehensive, robust (non-statistical), nuanced, notable,
  noteworthy, invaluable, meticulous(ly), multifaceted, seamless, compelling,
  unprecedented, profound, groundbreaking, transformative, holistic,
  paramount, landscape, realm, tapestry, interplay, insights, avenue,
  testament, synergy, paradigm (non-technical), plethora, myriad.
  Rule: > P90 or > 3 per 1000 words.
- **L-02** atmosphere words: quietly, silent, subtle, hidden, overlooked,
  underappreciated, little-noticed — any non-technical use flagged.
- **L-03** reaction adverbs: remarkably, strikingly, intriguingly,
  surprisingly, interestingly, importantly, notably, crucially, critically,
  indeed, clearly, fundamentally, "it is worth noting" — > 1 per 1000; every
  sentence-initial use flagged.
- **L-04** agent-style vocabulary: load-bearing, honest(ly), genuinely,
  cleanly, "a sharp test", the crux, "the real X", "what X actually
  measures", lands, earns its place, surfaces (verb), at the heart of —
  any occurrence flagged.
- **L-05** evaluative adjective with no number/σ/comparison in the sentence.
- **L-06** vague abstract nouns (landscape, realm, tapestry, interplay,
  insights, implications, dynamics (non-physics), complexities, perspectives,
  avenue, testament, synergy) — > P90.
- **L-07** formulaic collocations: robust framework, comprehensive
  analysis/overview, valuable/key insights, important implications,
  complex/intricate interplay, significant advancement, novel approach,
  fundamental/deeper/nuanced understanding, critical/crucial/pivotal role,
  growing body, promising avenue, holistic view — any occurrence.
- **L-08** inflated register (Latinate where plain exists) — ratio of 3+
  syllable words outside technical terms, > P90.
- **L-09** synonym rotation for one referent (sample/population/cohort;
  candidate→PN; detection→source). One fixed term per entity; rotation that
  changes implied classification is always flagged.
- **L-10** uniform hedging lexicon + stacked hedges ("may potentially",
  "could perhaps suggest") — stacked always flagged.
- **L-11** missing field register (density of subfield terms/abbrevs < P10).
- **L-12** lexical diversity (MTLD, never raw TTR) outside P10–P90.

### 5.2 Phrase formulas (P) — hard-zero unless noted
- **P-01** contrastive reframing ("it's not X, it's Y", "X, not Y", "the
  question is not…") — max 1 in the paper, none in the Abstract. (But a
  *technically essential* distinction is preserved — see adversarial case 1.)
- **P-02** "the real/deeper/more fundamental/quieter question" — 0.
- **P-03** bare importance ("This matters because…", "This distinction is
  important") — 0 unless a consequence is shown.
- **P-04** reaction framing ("This result is striking") — 0.
- **P-05** pseudo-clefts/drum-rolls ("What limits the search is…", "The key
  point is…", "The answer is simple:") — 0.
- **P-06** colon reveals ("This leaves one possibility: …") — > P90; re-check
  after em-dash removal (colons inherit the job).
- **P-07** artificial revelation ("what emerges", "a deeper truth", "a
  pattern becomes apparent") — 0.
- **P-08** rhetorical questions — 0 in MNRAS research papers.
- **P-09** universal significance ("crucial for advancing our knowledge of
  the Universe") — 0.
- **P-10** stock frames: plays a crucial role in; sheds light on; paves the
  way for; remains an open question; a growing body of evidence; in recent
  years; has garnered significant attention; in the context of; a deeper
  understanding of; serves as; lays the groundwork; at the forefront of;
  taken together; against this backdrop — > P90.
- **P-11** opening connectives (Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, In
  addition, Overall, Ultimately, In conclusion, Taken together) —
  sentence-initial rate > P90.
- **P-12** signposting ("In this section we will…", "Having established X we
  now turn to Y", "Building upon these findings") — > P90.
- **P-13** triplets (three-item coordinated lists) — rate > P90; a
  three-part list whose length is set by content, not rhythm, is kept.
- **P-14** parallelism without quantities ("improves sensitivity, increases
  completeness, enhances reliability") — every case.
- **P-15** predictable endings ("…for future research", "…of the underlying
  processes", "…in the broader context") — > P90.
- **P-16** manufactured surprise ("Surprisingly" with no stated prior
  expectation contradicted) — every case.
- **P-17** "paradigm shift", "game-changing" — only if Stage 1 shows an
  established model is contradicted.

### 5.3 Sentence (S)
- **S-01** uniform sentence length: CV per section < P10 (provisional < 0.40).
- **S-02** short sentences for effect: ≤7 words, no number/symbol/citation,
  paragraph-final or consecutive; verbless fragments. > 1 per section; every
  fragment. (A short *factual* sentence — "We detect no source above 5σ" — is
  kept: judge by semantic function, not length.)
- **S-03** trailing commentary clause (", highlighting/underscoring/
  suggesting/indicating/demonstrating/emphasising/reflecting/revealing/
  providing/pointing to…") — > P90; every clause adding an unsupported
  inference.
- **S-04** repetitive sentence openings — > P90.
- **S-05** recurring syntactic templates (POS-skeleton clustering; e.g.
  "These [results] [highlight] the [importance] of [NP]") — > P90; Methods/
  Results exempt for reporting templates.
- **S-06** over-balanced "While X, Y, Z-ing W" symmetry — > P90.
- **S-07** concession on every result ("Although X, Y", "Despite X, Y") — > P90.
- **S-08** noun stacking (5+ pre-modifiers, 3+ prepositional chains, dense
  NPs holding several numbers) — every case.
- **S-09** parenthetical over-qualification (>1 caveat per sentence) — every case.
- **S-10** nested subordination depth > P95.
- **S-11** self-contained sentences with low cohesion (few anaphoric links,
  low lexical overlap with previous sentence) — paragraph mean < P10.
- **S-12** even cadence: clause counts and abstract-noun endings — outside
  P10–P90.
- **S-13** mechanical short/long alternation introduced by an earlier
  rewrite pass (over-correction tell).

### 5.4 Paragraph (G)
- **G-01** uniform paragraph length CV < P10 (provisional < 0.35).
- **G-02** five-part template paragraph (claim → elaboration → support →
  interpretation → significance) in > 40% of a section's paragraphs.
- **G-03** closing gloss: delete the final sentence; if no measurement,
  qualification, inference or needed transition is lost, it is a gloss.
  "These results highlight…" always flagged.
- **G-04** generic topic sentences ("An important aspect of…") — every case.
- **G-05** restating instead of developing (embedding similarity > 0.85, no
  new proposition).
- **G-06** paragraph not linked to the previous in the argument graph — > P90.
- **G-07** equal space for unequal evidence (decisive vs minor result).
- **G-08** "Taken together" closures — max 1 per major section.

### 5.5 Document (D)
- **D-01** introduction funnel opening on the field, not the problem.
- **D-02** interchangeable motivation (would the opening fit another paper
  on the same topic?).
- **D-03** unresolved question never stated in one sentence.
- **D-04** background longer than the gap warrants.
- **D-05** Methods as philosophy (no parameters/versions/thresholds).
- **D-06** interpretation before measurement in Results.
- **D-07** Discussion > 50% restatement of Results.
- **D-08** generic limitations paragraph.
- **D-09** Conclusions paraphrasing the Abstract instead of the principal
  quantitative findings.
- **D-10** the same number in the same wording in 3+ sections.
- **D-11** over-documentation of process/verification mechanics in the main
  text (the defensive agent style) — move to appendix/code release, keeping
  what reproducibility genuinely needs.
- **D-12** symmetric sections (same length/structure, parallel headings).
- **D-13** concepts re-introduced in multiple sections.
- **D-14** artificially comprehensive discussion (mechanisms outside the
  observed parameter range).
- **D-15** uniform register across Methods/Results/Discussion.

### 5.6 Semantic redundancy (Y)
Embed sentences; pairs with cosine > 0.80 within a section / > 0.85 across
sections go to an NLI/LLM judge: equivalent / complementary / contradictory /
more specific / more general / unrelated. Flag equivalents (Y-01);
contradictions are severity 1 (Y-02). Abstract and Conclusions summarising
main results is legitimate repetition.

### 5.7 Voice (V)
- **V-01** no authorial position on competing interpretations.
- **V-02** no history of the work (reads as planned perfectly) — report
  real deviations *if they are in the record*; never invent them (I-5).
- **V-03** grand framing beside level 1–2 claims.
- **V-04** style distance from the author's own papers (function words,
  Burrows' Delta).
- **V-05** one fingerprint across all sections (stylometric distance between
  sections < P10 of within-paper distances).
- **V-06** general-interest language ("the fascinating world of").

### 5.8 Surface (A)
Em-dashes as dramatic pivots (> P90 of the author's own usage; fix the
construction, don't swap for colons); semicolon rate > P90; scare quotes;
mixed UK/US spelling (MNRAS = British); hyphenation inconsistency
(multifrequency, non-thermal); mixed conventions (data is/are, km/s vs
km s⁻¹, tense drift in Methods); Markdown/chat artefacts (**bold**, "Note:",
"Certainly", bullets, "[citation needed]"); Unicode issues (minus sign,
curly quotes, zero-width spaces); version strings/internal IDs in prose;
numbers starting sentences as digits.

### 5.9 Statistical LM features (M) — supporting evidence only
Perplexity per section vs corpus; surprisal variance (burstiness) < P10;
Binoculars-style ratio (report only); function-word profile; POS n-gram
entropy < P10; repeated 3–6-grams outside protected terms > P90. Never
threshold on these alone; they penalise non-native writers and formulaic
sections.

### 5.10 Co-occurrence score (C) — main rewrite trigger
Slide a 4-sentence window; count distinct L/P/S/G feature IDs firing:
- C-01: ≥3 distinct features ⇒ mandatory rewrite of the window.
- C-02: 2 ⇒ review; rewrite only if it also fails Stage 1 or reads badly.
- C-03: revised paper must not exceed P75 of corpus windows in any section.

### 5.11 Cross-document (X) — if earlier papers from the same pipeline exist
Same introduction sequence (X-01), abstract structure (X-02), interchangeable
conclusion language (X-03), the same unusual adjectives/collocations across
unrelated topics (X-04), same transitions at same positions (X-05).
A consistent style is normal for one author; the question is whether
recurring phrases come from the writing system rather than the author.

### 5.12 Known pipeline habits — check these FIRST
1. contrastive reframing several times per page, including abstracts;
2. short dramatic closing sentences ("No candidate survives.");
3. bare significance statements and pseudo-clefts;
4. over-compressed noun phrases holding several numbers/qualifiers;
5. parenthetical/dash caveats on nearly every claim;
6. defensive process/reproducibility description in the main text;
7. numbers drifting between sections; one number in two roles;
8. table columns impossible by their own definitions;
9. a central claim hard to state in one sentence because every result gets
   equal weight.

### 5.13 MANDATORY measured battery (v2.0)

Every row is computed on sentence-segmented body text (captions analysed
separately), recorded before and after, and carries the stated provisional
threshold. Rows marked † were missed or under-weighted in the 2026-10-17 first
run and exist because of it.

**Lexical / word level**
| # | Metric | Provisional threshold |
|---|--------|----------------------|

| B-02 | L-01 excess-vocabulary lexicon rate | ≤ 0.3 (hard words 0) |
| B-03 | L-03 reaction-adverb rate | ≤ 1 |
| B-04 | L-04 agent-vocabulary hits | 0 |
| B-05 | "rather than" rate (post-fix relocation) | ≤ 2 |
| B-06 † | Reasoning-connective rate (therefore, thus, hence, accordingly, consequently, so sentence-initial) | ≤ 4 |

| B-09 † | Reporting-verb uniformity: distinct verbs among gives/yields/shows/holds/carries/reaches/returns; one verb > 40% of reporting slots | flag |

| B-18 | Paragraph-length CV (G-01); **B-18b paragraph-opener top share** | CV ≥ 0.35; opener ≤ 35% |
| B-19 | Closing-gloss sentences (G-03) | 0 |
| B-20 † | Caption template uniformity: share of captions sharing one syntactic template; **caption first-word share (brace-matched extraction — `[^{}]*` regexes silently miss captions with nested braces)** and paragraph-opener top share measured alongside | first-word top share ≤ 45%; para-opener ≤ 35% |
| B-21 † | Statistical over-reporting: (a) p-values per 1000 words; (b) full enumerations restated in the Summary/Conclusions that already appear itemised in the body (D-10 extended); (c) per-member statistic counts duplicated between prose and tables | (a) ≤ 2.5; (b) 0; (c) 0 |
| B-22 † | Hedge census with decomposition: total qualifiers per 1000, split into cross-references, distinct technical senses, and repeated same-sense hedges | total ≤ 2; repeated same-sense hedges 0 |
| B-23 † | Near-duplicate sentences: pairwise ≥ 75% content-word overlap (lexical complement to Y-01 embeddings — run BOTH) | 0 outside Abstract/Conclusions recap |
| B-24 | Repeated prose 6-grams ≥ 3 occurrences | 0 outside headings/protected terms |
| B-25 † | Section-shape uniformity: every section ending in a mini-summary paragraph, identical subsection skeletons (D-12 sharpened) | inspect; vary or waive |

**Post-fix guards**
| # | Metric | Provisional threshold |
|---|--------|----------------------|
| B-26 † | Punctuation-density deltas vs original (colons, semicolons, parentheses) after dash removal | each ≤ +3 per 1000 words |
| B-27 | Over-correction check (I-7): mechanical length alternation, register flattening, synonym rotation | 0 |
| B-28 † | Protected-term scan (5.0 item 5) actually executed with zero false flags | pass |

**How to run it.** One Python script over the segmented text, emitting a
markdown table — the reference implementation is `astra_core/deai/deai_battery.py`
(this file's canonical home; run it from any working directory with the edited
tex, plus the original tex
as an optional second argument). Store as `deai_battery.md` (before) and re-run for after. The
audit report reproduces the merged before/after table verbatim. If a metric
cannot be computed (e.g. no captions), the report says so — a blank row is a
waived row, and waived rows are visible. Run the after-pass with the ORIGINAL
tex as the second argument (`python3 deai_battery.py <edited.tex> <original.tex>`)
so B-26 is scored as a measured delta, not an absolute count. Caption
extraction must be brace-matched — `[^{}]*` regexes silently miss every caption
containing nested braces and under-count B-20 (this bug shipped once and
hid a 74%-uniform caption set).

## 6. Scoring

Never produce an AI-authorship probability. Three separate 0–100 scores with
underlying measurements:
- **A. Stylistic conventionality** — fraction of Section 5 metrics inside
  P10–P90, weighted to the author's corpus, per section.
- **B. Communication quality** — F-NOFUNC rate, redundancy, information
  density (checkable propositions per word), operational definitions.
- **C. Scientific integrity** — Stages 1–2; any severity-1 finding caps C
  at 50 until resolved.

Severity: **1** error/unsupported claim changing the science (fix or author
approval before submission); **2** defect a referee would raise; **3**
clarity/machine-pattern defect; **4** minor.

## 7. Stage 4 — controlled revision

Order of work, largest scale to smallest; re-run relevant checks after each
step:

1. **Science** (Stage 1–2 outputs): never fix by wording what needs analysis
   — author_decisions.md gets it. Fix wording-level defects: align claims to
   the epistemic ladder (weakening only, approved), define operational terms
   using values already in the record, correct false precision, attach
   citations to specific claims.
2. **Structure**: rebuild Abstract, first/last Introduction paragraphs and
   Conclusions around the main-finding sentence. Open the Introduction on
   the specific problem. Rewrite literature as argument (who found what,
   where results disagree, what is missing, why this paper exists). Cut
   Discussion text that repeats Results. Move process documentation to
   appendix/code. Replace generic limitations/future work with specific ones.
3. **Paragraphs**: delete F-NOFUNC paragraphs and closing glosses; one
   argument step per paragraph; one- and two-sentence paragraphs allowed;
   more space for decisive results, less for minor ones.
4. **Sentences**: remove excess contrastive reframes, pseudo-clefts, colon
   reveals, rhetorical questions, drum-rolls, trailing commentary clauses;
   fold short dramatic sentences into ordinary statements (keep short
   factual ones); decompress noun stacks; move caveats out of parentheses.
5. **Words**: replace evaluations with the measurement or delete; remove
   reaction adverbs, atmosphere words, agent vocabulary; one term per
   entity; build logic into the sentence instead of opening connectives;
   journal-standard spelling/units/hyphenation.

Rewriting rules:
- **W-01** specific beats sophisticated: "These intriguing results provide
  compelling evidence for a new population" → "29 of the 37 inverted-spectrum
  sources have no previous classification."
- **W-02** delete rather than re-fill.
- **W-03** repeat technical terms; never synonym-substitute for variety.
- **W-04** never strengthen; "consistent with" never becomes "demonstrates".
- **W-05** sentence length varies because content varies, never mechanically.
- **W-06** the argument supplies transitions.
- **W-07** keep section conventions (Methods reproducible, Results factual,
  Discussion interpretive, Conclusions selective).
- **W-08** replace novelty/significance claims with verifiable comparisons.
- **W-09** keep legitimate Abstract/Conclusions repetition.
- **W-10** write in the author's voice: sentence-length profile, preferred
  connectives, "we" usage, stance verbs, from their prior papers.
- **W-11** take a position where the evidence supports one: "We favour X
  because Y; Z is disfavoured at Nσ."
- **W-12** MNRAS house style unless told otherwise.

## 8. Stage 5 — verification gates

- **T-00 (v2.0, first):** the 5.13 battery table exists with before AND after
  values for every row; no row is blank without a written waiver. A run
  failing T-00 is reported to the author as incomplete, not as done.

Run in a FRESH agent context (not the one that revised):
- **T-01** protected-token diff empty apart from approved change-log entries.
- **T-02** every original proposition maps to an equivalent, or is logged as
  removed redundancy; no new propositions.
- **T-03** no claim higher on the epistemic ladder; lowerings approved.
- **T-04** N-01…N-07 pass.
- **T-05** R-01 passes; LaTeX compiles clean, no undefined refs/citations;
  readable PDF left behind.
- **T-06** main finding: a fresh agent reading only Abstract/Intro/Conclusions
  states the finding in one sentence with a number; must match 3.1.
- **T-07** style metrics inside range (or provisional limits); hard-zero
  features zero (P-02/03/04/05/07/09, L-04); P-01 ≤ 1.
- **T-08** no co-occurrence window ≥ 3; C-03 ≤ P75 per section.
- **T-09** no over-correction tactics present (Section 1, I-7).
- **T-10** adversarial/edge cases pass: technical contrasts preserved;
  protected terms unflagged; short factual sentences kept; same number in
  different units reconciled not contradicted; a valid but AI-looking human
  paragraph left alone; a fluent paragraph with wrong physics still flagged.
- **T-11** journal limits (MNRAS abstract 250 words single paragraph; arXiv
  1920-char metadata; no unexplained acronyms in title).
- **T-12** AI-assistance disclosure present where the journal requires it.
- **T-13** author sign-off: author reads Abstract, Introduction and
  Conclusions aloud; nothing submitted with an open severity-1 item.

Stop after three full cycles; hand residual failures to the author.

## 9. Outputs (into the paper's version directory)

1. `manuscript_revised.tex` (+ all subsidiary files), compiling cleanly, with
   fresh `.pdf`.
2. `deai_change_log.md` — every change: old text → new text, feature ID /
   defect ID, justification, approval status.
3. `deai_audit_report.md` — main-finding sentence + pass/fail; the three
   scores; severity-1 and -2 findings with quotes and fixes; per-section
   style metric summary; co-occurrence stats.
4. `deai_author_decisions.md` — science defects, weakened claims, missing
   content needing the author.
5. `deai_invariant_check.md` — token-register diff, proposition map,
   numerical/citation checks, gate results.

## 10. Referee correspondence (reference only — standing directive: no
response letters are produced unless explicitly requested)

Thank once, briefly, at the top. Never "we thank the referee for this
insightful comment" / "the referee is absolutely right". Per point: quote →
what changed → where (section/line/equation) → new text if short.
Disagree plainly with evidence. Any claimed change must exist in the
manuscript. Same Stage 3 checks apply to the letter.

## Quick-reference: the ten things to check first (if time is short)

**v2.0 note: "if time is short" is no longer an accepted mode — the battery is
mandatory. This list is the order of first attack, not a permitted subset.**

1. The main-finding sentence exists and is used to organise Abstract/Concl.
2. Contrastive reframing count; short dramatic closers; pseudo-clefts.
3. Closing glosses ("These results highlight…") — delete.
4. Evaluative adjectives without numbers — replace with the number.
5. Trailing ", suggesting…" clauses adding unsupported inference.
6. Uniform sentence length and paragraph rhythm (read aloud test).
7. Number consistency across abstract/text/tables/captions.
8. Claim strength drift Results → Conclusions.
9. Defensive process narration in the main text → appendix.
10. Literature as list vs argument; decorative citation blocks.

Both source analyses converge on the same conclusion, which is the protocol's
one-line summary: **a paper stops reading as machine-written when every
sentence is doing scientific work — measurement, constraint, comparison or
decision — in the author's own settled terms.** There is no shortcut below
that, and no trick that substitutes for it.
